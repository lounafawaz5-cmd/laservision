# How to use this document

This is the marketing strategy for both brands: LaserVision and the
ZEISS Vision Center. It sets the direction before any content is
planned. Once Marc and Georges approve it and answer the questions at
the end, Tawlé and Luna build the content calendar and Luna starts
producing.

The order matters. Content that starts from ideas produces scattered
posts. Content that starts from a clear brand personality, a clear
audience and a clear job produces accounts people follow. We built the
direction first and reverse-engineer the calendar from it.

**WHAT IS IN HERE**

- Where we are: what discovery told us and what changes now

- The two brands as one system: roles, goals, audiences, and how they
  hand customers to each other

- LaserVision: positioning, messaging, personality, faces, content
  system, channels, doctor track

- ZEISS Vision Center: positioning, messaging, personality, face,
  content system, seasonal anchors, conversion path

- Beyond content: website, database, WhatsApp, doctor programme,
  long-term moves

- Roles, action list in three tiers, and the questions that need answers

**WHAT IS NOT IN HERE**

The content calendar. It comes next week, built on the approved
direction and on the answers to the questions in section 10.

## What changes now

Luna is in-house and on site. That changes the model. Instead of an
agency delivering a monthly batch, LaserVision and ZVC now have someone
who is there every day, can film when something happens, and knows the
staff. The strategy is designed for that: frequent, phone-shot,
personality-led content that a single person can sustain.

Tawlé's role is to give Luna the direction, the formats and the
calendar, get the direction approved, then move into ongoing support.
The LaserVision team decides which topics matter; Tawlé and Luna decide
how they are delivered. This document is the direction.

# One system, two brands

LaserVision and the ZEISS Vision Center share a building, a team and a
customer base. They do not share a voice. Each has a distinct job, and
the system works when each does its own job well and hands customers to
the other at the right moment.

|                             | **LaserVision**                                                                                                                                 | **ZEISS Vision Center**                                                                                              |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **What it is**              | **A 30-year-old eye care and laser platform. Four locations. Doctors operate here; patients are treated here.**                                 | **A retail optical store inside the Hazmieh branch. ZEISS lenses, frames from multiple brands, eye tests, styling.** |
| **Business model**          | **Services. Revenue mainly from doctors renting rooms and equipment, plus direct patients across eye care, aesthetic laser and audiology.**     | **Retail. Revenue from glasses, lenses and related products.**                                                       |
| **Marketing goal**          | **Awareness and positioning. Become the reference for eye care and laser in Lebanon, so patients want the platform their doctor already uses.** | **Sales. Make ZEISS a Lebanese store people walk into, not an international brand they scroll past.**                |

## The loop

Every patient who needs glasses after an eye consultation at LaserVision
is sent to ZEISS. Every ZEISS customer who needs an eye test or
discovers a medical issue is sent to LaserVision. The content reflects
this: LaserVision mentions ZEISS as where you get your glasses; ZEISS
mentions LaserVision as where you get your eyes checked. In-clinic
signage and ads, already being worked on with ZEISS, close the loop
physically.

# LaserVision

## Positioning

**The platform Lebanon's eye doctors trust.**

This sentence does three things. It states what LaserVision is (a
platform, not a doctor). It borrows credibility from the people patients
already trust (their doctor). And it turns the two hardest constraints,
no doctors on camera and always consult your doctor first, into the
message itself.

**PROMISE TO THE PATIENT**

You will understand every step before it happens. Nothing here is a
surprise.

**PROOF**

- Over 30 years in operation, founded in 1995

- Four locations: Hazmieh (Jisr El Basha), Tripoli, Saida and Hôtel-Dieu
  de France

- Partnership with HDF since 2020

- The equipment, the rooms, the sterilisation process, the team

- The number of doctors who choose to operate here

**KEY MESSAGES**

1.  Your doctor decides what you need. We make sure it happens safely
    and clearly.

2.  Everything you want to know about a procedure, answered before you
    walk in.

3.  The same standard in every branch, from Tripoli to Saida.

4.  More than eyes: laser treatments and hearing care under one roof.

5.  Thirty years of doing this, and still explaining it like it is your
    first time.

**WHAT LASERVISION NEVER SAYS**

- Doctor names or comparisons between doctors

- Promises about results or outcomes

- Fear-based language about what happens if you do not act

- "Best in Lebanon", "first in Lebanon" or similar without a claim that
  has been cleared

- Retail or discount language

## Two audiences, two tracks

LaserVision's revenue comes mainly from doctors, but doctors do not
follow clinics on Instagram. This is not a contradiction. Social media
is for patients. Its job is to make LaserVision the name patients
recognise, trust and ask for, because that is what makes a surgeon
choose to operate here. Patient-facing content is the doctor pitch,
delivered indirectly.

### Educational, 50%

LaserVision has more services than any competitor and almost none of
them are explained anywhere. Every service produces the same set of
questions, which gives Luna a repeatable structure. For each service:
what is it, who is it for, how to prepare, what happens after, and what
people get wrong about it.

**EXAMPLES**

- Reception, to camera: "You've booked a laser session. Here's what to
  bring, what to skip, and how long you'll be here."

- Technician, hands and machine: "What actually happens during a laser
  hair removal session, from the first pass to the last."

- Georges: "Your doctor said cataract. Here's what happens next, in
  order."

- Nurse: "Three things people do the night before refractive surgery
  that they shouldn't."

- Audiology: "How do you know it's time for a hearing test? Five signs,
  none of them dramatic."

- Myth vs fact: "Laser eye surgery hurts." Thirty seconds, one answer.

### Credibility, 30%

This pillar makes the platform visible. Most patients do not know the
range of services, the four locations, the HDF partnership or how the
rooms are run. Safety and hygiene live here, not in the story pillar.

**EXAMPLES**

- Nurse, hands only, no voice: "This is how we clean the room between
  patients." Text on screen, 25 seconds.

- "Tripoli branch: how to find us, where to park, who'll meet you at the
  door." One per branch.

- "What this machine does." Equipment explained through the screen and
  the hands, not a talking head.

- "Everything we do, in one carousel." The full service map, pinned.

- "Since 1995." Thirty years in five numbers: locations, patients,
  procedures, doctors, cities.

- HDF: what the partnership means for a patient, once we have confirmed
  what can be said publicly.

### Storytelling, 20%

The pillar that makes the account human. Marc's story is the anchor: how
it started, what went wrong, what changed, how Georges joined. Patient
stories are told lightly, first person, focused on the experience rather
than the medical detail. Testimonials belong here, but as moments, not
corporate videos.

**EXAMPLES**

- Marc: "In 1995 I had one machine and a rented room. Chapter one." A
  series, one chapter per month.

- Marc and Georges together: what changed when the son joined, what they
  disagree on.

- "A patient's day." From the door to the exit, lightly, with consent.
  Before and after where the service allows it.

- A staff member: how long they have been here and what they tell
  nervous patients.

- The Tripoli or Saida opening: why there, what it took.

## Pinned posts

## The doctor track

Small, separate, professional. Its job is to keep LaserVision top of
mind as the platform where doctors operate, and to give referring
doctors a reason to keep referring.

- A periodic email to operating and referring doctors: new equipment,
  capacity, branch news, one useful clinical or operational note.
  Quarterly to start.

- A one-page "why operate at LaserVision" sheet for direct outreach to
  surgeons who do not use the platform yet.

- LinkedIn reactivated: institutional voice, two posts a month, Marc as
  the author. Equipment, partnerships, milestones.

- A referral programme for doctors, structured once we know who refers
  today (questions E3 and E4 from discovery are still open).

# ZEISS Vision Center

## Positioning

**Frames you'll want to wear. Lenses that actually work.**

ZEISS is a global name with a reputation for precision. In Lebanon, it
needs to become a store: a place with a personality, a face and a reason
to visit. The positioning pairs the two things a customer wants from an
optician, to look good and to see well, and promises both in one visit.

**PROMISE TO THE CUSTOMER**

Walk in unsure. Walk out looking good and seeing clearly.

**PROOF**

- ZEISS lenses, with staff trained by ZEISS

- New products available here three months before other opticians

- Frames from multiple known brands, in one place

- Two optometrists on the floor, and an eye clinic next door if anything
  needs a doctor

- Styling help from people who do this all day

**KEY MESSAGES**

1.  Your face has a frame. We'll find it.

2.  Two pairs or one? Lenses that change with the light.

3.  Screens all day. Lenses made for that.

4.  New arrivals land here first.

5.  Eyes checked next door, glasses ready here.

**WHAT ZEISS VISION CENTER NEVER DOES**

- Uses the ZEISS name or logo in content the store produces (per the
  collaboration agreement)

- Makes medical claims or talks about surgery, conditions or treatment

- Lectures. Product knowledge is delivered in one sentence, then it
  moves on

- Hard-sells or runs constant discounts
